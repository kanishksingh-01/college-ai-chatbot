ABSTRACT

In modern collegiate environments, accessing real-time, authoritative academic information remains a persistent challenge for students and administrative personnel alike. Students constantly encounter confusion regarding multi-campus locations, course fee structures, admission criteria, lecture schedules, and institutional policies. A critical area of academic anxiety is the complex attendance evaluation mechanism, where students struggle to determine their real-time standing against university detention thresholds across theory and laboratory subjects.

Traditional campus inquiry channels depend heavily on physical noticeboards, in-person administrative visits, and rigid enterprise software portals that lack intuitive, conversational interfaces. To address these systemic bottlenecks, College AI Chatbot (GH Raisoni AI Campus Assistant) has been engineered as an intelligent, conversational, full-stack web application designed to deliver instantaneous, verified institutional assistance.

The system seamlessly integrates natural language keyword classification with structured database querying, offering instant resolutions for campus inquiries spanning the Wagholi Campus (Gat 1200, Domkhel Rd, Pune 412207) and the Yerawada Campus (Nyati Unitree East-Wing, Airport Rd, Pune 411006). A centerpiece innovation is the automated 4-Tier Attendance Detention Engine, which dynamically classifies student attendance into Safe (≥75%), Warning (60%–74.9%), Critical (50%–59.9%), and Severe Detention (<50%) tiers, complete with specific institutional action rubrics.

Furthermore, the platform incorporates bi-directional voice interaction utilizing the browser Web Speech API for speech-to-text recognition and server-side Google Text-to-Speech (gTTS) for natural voice playback. An administrative dashboard empowers staff to update FAQs, audit query logs, and export comprehensive CSV/PDF analytical reports, creating a unified, reliable, and accessible campus ecosystem.

INTRODUCTION:

In today’s fast-changing and competitive higher education landscape, university campuses are vibrant hubs of academic, extracurricular, and administrative activities. Institutions cater to thousands of students across diverse disciplines including Computer Applications, Engineering, Management, and Sciences. With such scale comes a massive volume of recurring student inquiries regarding admission criteria, semester fees, lecture timetables, examination schedules, syllabus details, faculty contacts, and multi-campus transportation routes.

Traditional university helpdesks and administrative inquiry counters are heavily overburdened, leading to long queues, delayed responses, and student dissatisfaction. Staff members are repeatedly engaged in answering identical routine questions, detracting from critical administrative duties. Furthermore, conventional student portals are often text-heavy, non-intuitive on mobile devices, and require multiple navigation steps to locate basic institutional details.

With the rapid emergence of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Natural Language Processing (NLP), conversational AI assistants have transformed modern user experiences. AI-powered chatbots provide round-the-clock, real-time, interactive responses, democratizing information access while reducing administrative overhead. By analyzing user intent from free-form text or spoken voice inputs, intelligent agents can instantly locate relevant knowledge records and provide verified, context-aware answers.

College AI Chatbot is specifically designed for G H Raisoni International Skill Tech University, Pune. It serves as an institutional digital guide, bridging the communication gap between students and university authorities. The system centralizes campus inquiries, delivers verified Pune campus coordinates for both Wagholi and Yerawada locations, provides real-time attendance calculations against the official 4-tier detention policy, and offers high-accessibility voice interaction.

PROBLEM DEFINITION :

In modern multi-campus university environments like G H Raisoni International Skill Tech University, students face notable hurdles in retrieving accurate, prompt, and personalized academic information. A major issue is the geographical confusion surrounding dual campuses in Pune: students frequently mix up department locations between the Wagholi Campus (Gat 1200, Domkhel Rd) and the Yerawada Campus (Nyati Unitree East-Wing, Airport Rd). The lack of an instant, accessible geolocation and address verification portal causes travel delays and missed academic deadlines.

A more severe issue lies in attendance monitoring and detention policy transparency. University regulations mandate strict attendance thresholds for semester examination eligibility. However, students typically discover their attendance standing only at the end of the semester when physical detention lists are displayed on departmental noticeboards. At that late stage, students with attendance falling between 50% and 74.9% have already crossed the threshold into remedial assignments, parent hearings, or course debarment without prior warning.

Furthermore, traditional campus inquiry systems suffer from distinct structural limitations:

  • Administrative Queue Bottlenecks: Student service desks are accessible only during fixed office hours (10:00 AM to 5:00 PM), leaving evening and weekend inquiries completely unattended.
  • Absence of Voice-Assisted Accessibility: Visually impaired students or students on mobile devices face friction navigating complex tabular web portals.
  • Fragmented Information Silos: Syllabi, fee installment rules, bus schedules, and exam guidelines are dispersed across disconnected noticeboards and PDFs.
  • Lack of Centralized Audit & Reporting: Administrative coordinators lack unified telemetry to track common student doubts, trending academic queries, or batch-wide detention risk patterns in downloadable formats.

EXISTING SYSTEM :

The existing information dissemination framework at the university relies primarily on traditional methods, physical noticeboards, in-person administrative visits, and static enterprise ERP web pages. Inquiries regarding admissions, fees, examination timetables, and campus addresses require students to physically visit the administration block or call departmental helplines during working hours.

For attendance, faculty members record daily attendance manually or in centralized spreadsheets. Attendance percentages are compiled periodically and posted as printed circulars outside HOD offices. There is no automated, conversational self-service portal where a student can type or speak their query and instantly obtain personalized attendance standing, detention tier classification, or remedial guidance. Overall, the existing approach is time-consuming, prone to delays, resource-heavy, and non-interactive.

NEED FOR NEW SYSTEM :

Modern students expect immediate, mobile-first, conversational digital services. To eliminate operational inefficiencies and provide round-the-clock accessibility, an intelligent software solution is required. The key motivations for developing the new system include:

  • Instant 24/7 Availability: Immediate answers to frequent college inquiries without queuing or calling staff.
  • Multi-Campus Geographic Clarity: Clear, unambiguous campus addresses and location coordinates for Wagholi and Yerawada premises.
  • Automated Detention Risk Assessment: Immediate 4-tier classification (≥75% Safe, 60%–74.9% Warning, 50%–59.9% Critical, <50% Severe) providing actionable warnings early in the semester.
  • Multimodal Voice Interface: Integration of browser speech recognition and natural text-to-speech voice synthesis for hands-free and inclusive interaction.
  • Administrative Governance: An admin console with real-time audit logs and CSV export capabilities for academic monitoring.

REQUIREMENT ANALYSIS

Requirement analysis is a vital software engineering phase where user expectations, system capabilities, and operational constraints are defined. For the College AI Chatbot, requirements were derived by evaluating daily student inquiries at GH Raisoni University and classifying them into functional and non-functional specifications.

1. Functional Requirements :
  • Student Authentication: The system must securely validate student roll numbers (e.g. ISTU25010120001) to access personalized student profile and attendance records.
  • Multi-Campus Geographic Guidance: The system must accurately differentiate between Wagholi and Yerawada campuses, returning verified addresses and landmarks.
  • Attendance Calculation: The system must retrieve raw student attendance data and compute current percentage figures.
  • 4-Tier Detention Classification: The system must evaluate attendance against the institutional 4-tier rubric (Safe ≥75%, Warning 60-74.9%, Critical 50-59.9%, Severe <50%) and display exact academic directives.
  • Multimodal Voice I/O: The system must accept voice input via microphone and deliver audio speech output via gTTS.
  • Feedback Collection: The system must provide simple feedback thumbs up/down icons to record answer satisfaction.
  • Admin Knowledge Management & Reporting: Staff must be able to add/edit FAQs and export chat logs and detention summaries in CSV format.
2. Non-Functional Requirements :
  • Performance & Latency: System response time for FAQ retrieval and attendance lookup must not exceed 250 milliseconds.
  • Availability & Reliability: The service must provide 99.9% uptime during semester examination and admission periods.
  • Usability & Accessibility: The user interface must be clean, mobile-responsive, and intuitive with high contrast text.
  • Security & Data Integrity: SQL injection vulnerabilities must be mitigated via parameterized queries; student data must remain protected.

TESTING AND IMPLEMENTATION :
TESTING STRATEGIES:
1. Top-Down Strategy: Testing begins from top-level user interface forms and navigation routes, verifying user inputs and simulated mock responses before integrating database models.
2. Bottom-Up Strategy: Low-level database procedures, SQLite schema constraints, and gTTS audio helper functions are unit-tested independently prior to integration into Flask endpoints.
3. Incremental Testing Strategy: Features are implemented and validated step-by-step (e.g., student login → keyword search → attendance lookup → detention logic → voice audio synthesis).
4. System Testing Strategy: The entire integrated web application is tested holistically under real-world scenarios to verify response latency, concurrent student sessions, and CSV report export integrity.
TESTING TECHNIQUES:
1. Black Box Testing: Functionality is validated strictly against functional requirements without inspecting internal code logic. Techniques include Equivalence Partitioning and Boundary Value Analysis on attendance cutoffs (75.0%, 74.9%, 60.0%, 59.9%, 50.0%, 49.9%).
2. White Box Testing: Internal code structure, execution paths, SQL parameterized statements, and regex keyword matcher loops are verified for branch coverage and error handling.

DRAWBACKS AND LIMITATIONS :

While the College AI Chatbot provides comprehensive, real-time campus assistance and automated detention governance, like any software system, it operates under certain inherent technical and operational limitations:

1. Dependency on Predefined Knowledge Base :
The chatbot utilizes regex keyword matching and relational FAQ indexing. If a student query uses highly non-standard slang, complex colloquial phrasing, or concerns a novel topic not registered in the SQLite knowledge base, the system falls back to a default guidance response rather than synthesizing new answers through generative AI.
2. Client-Side Speech Recognition Vulnerability :
The voice input feature relies upon the browser Web Speech API. Ambient background noise in crowded campus corridors or poor microphone quality can lead to transcription errors, requiring the student to re-speak or revert to keyboard typing.
3. Single-Tenant SQLite Database Concurrency :
The system currently deploys SQLite 3 as its primary persistence engine. While exceptionally fast for read-heavy operations, SQLite employs file-level database locking during write operations, which may create a performance bottleneck if thousands of students concurrently submit chat evaluations.
4. Internet Connectivity Requirement for gTTS :
While FAQ matching executes locally on the Python Flask server, synthesizing new audio files via Google Text-to-Speech (gTTS) requires outbound internet access. In the event of network disruption, voice output falls back to browser-native synthesis or text-only responses.

PROPOSED ENHANCEMENTS :

To build upon the successful implementation of the College AI Chatbot, several progressive architectural enhancements are planned for future versions:

1. Multilingual Support (Marathi, Hindi & English) :
Recognizing the regional demographic diversity across Pune and Maharashtra, the natural language understanding engine can be expanded to support Marathi and Hindi conversational queries and spoken voice responses, greatly enhancing accessibility for parents and rural students.
2. Local Open-Source LLM Integration (LLaMA / Gemini API) :
Upgrading the query matcher with a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architecture powered by an on-premise Large Language Model (e.g. LLaMA 3.2 or Gemini Flash API) will allow the chatbot to answer nuanced, multi-part academic questions by directly referencing institutional PDF handbooks.
3. Automated Guardian SMS & WhatsApp Alerts :
Integrating with institutional SMS/WhatsApp gateways (e.g., Twilio or Gupshup) to automatically dispatch early-warning attendance alerts directly to parents whenever a student enters Tier 2 (Warning) or Tier 3 (Critical) status.
4. Enterprise Database Migration & Native Mobile App :
Migrating the backend persistence layer from SQLite to PostgreSQL for high-concurrency scaling, and wrapping the frontend in a cross-platform Flutter/React Native application for Android and iOS devices.
